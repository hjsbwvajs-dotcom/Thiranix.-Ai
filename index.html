04 — Learn

Robots explained

Industrial robot arms

Multi-jointed arms bolted to a factory floor. They weld, paint, pick and assemble with speed and repeatable accuracy, and power most car and electronics lines.

Collaborative robots (cobots)

Smaller arms built to work next to people without safety cages, using force sensing to stop on contact. Popular with small factories that need flexible automation.

Mobile robots (AGVs and AMRs)

Wheeled robots that carry goods around warehouses and plants. AGVs follow fixed routes, while AMRs use sensors and AI to find their own way.

Humanoid robots

Robots with a human-like body, designed to work in spaces built for people. They are still an active research area, with early use in logistics and inspection.

Aerial robots (drones)

Flying robots used for surveying, crop monitoring, delivery and inspecting hard-to-reach structures such as towers and pipelines.

Medical robots

Systems that help surgeons operate with fine precision through small incisions, plus robots for rehabilitation, lab automation and hospital logistics.

Agricultural robots

Machines that plant, weed, spray and harvest. Vision-based AI lets them tell crops from weeds and treat each plant individually.

Service robots

Robots that help in everyday settings, such as vacuum cleaners at home, delivery robots in hotels and guide robots in airports.
